## The Character Brief

Before names, let's nail what this character IS.

**Core traits the character must embody:**
- Curiosity (drives the learning)
- Adaptability (changes form, grows with the child)
- Intelligence that doesn't feel condescending (smart friend, not teacher)
- Warmth (this is the child's companion, not a tool)
- Coolness that ages well (a 4yo says "cool!", a 12yo doesn't cringe)

**What makes this character different from every other edtech mascot:**
- Duolingo's owl is a notification machine with guilt mechanics
- ABCmouse uses generic cartoon animals
- Khan Academy's characters are static illustrations
- None of them *change*, *remember*, or *grow*

This character is alive in a way no edtech mascot has been. It shapeshifts. It remembers. It gets older when you do. It speaks differently to a 5-year-old than an 8-year-old — not because of a setting, but because it *knows* you.

## The Big Idea: A Character That Evolves

The character starts as one thing and can transform — but it always has a "true form" it returns to. This is the throughline across thousands of lessons and years of use.

### Concept A — The Cortex Character (Brain-Based)

The character is literally a stylized brain-creature. Not gross, not medical — think a small, glowing, bioluminescent being with neural-network patterns that pulse when it thinks. Friendly face. Floats.

**Why the brain works:**
- Directly ties to learning, intelligence, thinking
- Neural pathways lighting up = visual metaphor for "your brain is growing"
- The character's neural patterns get MORE complex as the child levels up (visible evolution)
- Anatomically educational — kids learn about their own brain through the character
- Unique in the market — no one has a brain mascot that's actually cool

**How it evolves with age:**

| Age 4-5 | Age 6-7 | Age 8-10 | Age 11-14 |
|---------|---------|----------|-----------|
| Round, simple, big eyes, few visible neural paths, bouncy movement, giggles | More defined features, neural patterns visible and pulsing, confident posture | Sleek, angular features emerging, complex neural web, uses slang naturally | Almost humanoid silhouette, intricate brain-circuit aesthetic, speaks like a smart older friend |

**Shapeshifting:** When teaching about animals, it morphs into a brain-version of that animal (a glowing neural elephant, a circuit-patterned dolphin). When teaching about space, it becomes a brain-shaped nebula. Always recognizable by its core glow color and eye shape. Snaps back to true form between lessons.

**Name options for this character:**
- **Syn** (from synapse) — short, punchy, gender-neutral, sounds like a friend's name
- **Axon** — the part of a neuron that sends signals. Cool-sounding, unique.
- **Lobe** — playful, slightly goofy for young kids, gets funnier as kids learn brain anatomy
- **Cort** (from cortex) — short, strong, ages well
- **Dendro** (from dendrite — the receiving end of a neuron) — unique, has a nature-feel too (dendro = tree in Greek)

### Concept B — The Shapeshifter (Adaptation-Based)

The character is made of a fluid, luminous material — like living light or intelligent liquid metal. No fixed animal or human form. Its "true form" is an abstract but friendly blob-like entity with expressive eyes and a core glow. Think Flubber meets Baymax's personality meets the T-1000's visual.

**Why shapeshifting works:**
- Embodies the core product: adaptation, change, evolution
- Every lesson feels fresh — the character becomes part of the topic
- Kids anticipate "what will it become this time?"
- The true form is iconic and simple enough for an app icon
- Gender-neutral, species-neutral, culture-neutral — universal appeal

**How it evolves with age:**

| Age 4-5 | Age 6-7 | Age 8-10 | Age 11-14 |
|---------|---------|----------|-----------|
| Blobby, squishy, round with big eyes, simple expressions, uses baby-ish enthusiasm | More defined shape, starts showing geometric edges, more personality in expressions | Can hold complex forms longer, shows off, does tricks with its transformations | Sleek, minimal, controlled — transforms with precision rather than bouncy chaos. Cool, understated. |

**Shapeshifting mechanics:**
- Teaching about robots → becomes a mini robot (but with its signature eyes and glow)
- Teaching about the ocean → becomes a glowing jellyfish form
- Teaching about code → becomes a pattern of flowing symbols that coalesce into its face
- Between topics → smoothly melts back to true form with a satisfying visual
- When the child gets an answer right → does an excited shape burst
- When the child struggles → becomes softer, rounder, more comforting

**Name options for this character:**
- **Morph** — literally what it does. Simple, memorable, fun to say. "Hey Morph!"
- **Flux** — change, flow, adaptation. Short, techy, cool at any age.
- **Shift** — direct, action-oriented, sounds like a cool friend
- **Proto** (from protean — able to change form) — techy, unique, "Hey Proto!"
- **Evo** (from evolution) — growth, change, getting better. "Hey Evo!"

### Concept C — The Neural Pet (Hybrid: Brain + Animal + Digital)

A creature that doesn't exist in nature — part animal, part digital, part organic brain-tissue. Imagine if a fox and a neural network had a baby, and that baby was made of light. It has ears (that are actually antenna-like neural extensions), a tail (that's a trailing chain of synaptic sparks), paws, and a face — but everything about it says "I'm from the future."

**Why a neural pet works:**
- Kids inherently bond with animal-like characters
- The "pet" framing creates a care relationship (not just a teacher)
- Neural/digital elements keep it techy and unique
- A pet that transforms is a beloved trope (Pokémon energy without the IP issues)
- Kid could name it or choose from variants (fox-type, cat-type, bird-type — each with the neural aesthetic)

**How it evolves with age:**

| Age 4-5 | Age 6-7 | Age 8-10 | Age 11-14 |
|---------|---------|----------|-----------|
| Small, puppy-like, big paws it hasn't grown into, stumbles cutely, circuit patterns are faint | Growing into its form, more graceful, circuit patterns glow brighter, developing unique features | Fully formed, elegant, powerful visual presence, complex circuit art across its body | Battle-ready aesthetic (think Tron), lean, confident, treats the kid as an equal |

**Variant selection (kid chooses at onboarding):**
- **Circuit Fox** — clever, quick, mischievous
- **Pulse Cat** — calm, curious, independent
- **Spark Owl** — wise, observant, encouraging
- **Byte Wolf** — brave, loyal, adventurous

All share: glowing neural-circuit patterns, signature eye style, same AI personality underneath. Different visual shell, same brain.

**Name options (for the species, not individual):**
- **Synth** (as a species name) — "My Synth is a fox type!" Synthetic + synapse
- **Neuron** / **Nuri** — cute shortening, sounds like a name
- **Link** — neural links, but also the connection between kid and AI
- **Kin** — family, connection, "a Kin" as a species name. "What's your Kin learning?"

## App Name — Separating Brand from Character

One key decision: **does the app name = the character name?**

**Option 1: Same name (like "Duolingo" = the app, Duo = the owl — close but not identical)**
- App: Cortex / Character: Cort
- App: Synapse / Character: Syn
- App: Evolve / Character: Evo

**Option 2: Separate names (like "Pokémon" = the brand, Pikachu = the character)**
- App: Kinetic / Character: Kin
- App: Dendrite / Character: Dendro
- App: Cortex / Character: [kid names their own]

**Option 3: The character IS the brand (like "Siri" or "Alexa")**
- Just "Flux" — the app is Flux, the character is Flux, the brand is Flux
- Just "Syn" — "Hey Syn, teach me about robots"
- Just "Proto" — the app, the character, the everything

For a kids' product, Option 3 is the strongest. One name. No confusion. A 4-year-old can say it. A parent can say "time for Flux" or "ask Syn." The name becomes synonymous with the experience.

## The Aging System — How the Character Grows

Regardless of which character wins, the aging mechanic is the same:

**The parent sets an age/level in Companion. The character responds.**

But it's not just a skin swap. The character's *personality* shifts:

| Dimension | Age 4-5 | Age 6-7 | Age 8-10 | Age 11-14 |
|-----------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Vocabulary** | Simple, repetitive, exclamatory | Expanded, some compound sentences | Full vocabulary, explains nuance | Slang-aware, references their interests |
| **Humor** | Silly sounds, pratfalls, surprise faces | Puns, wordplay, gentle teasing | Sarcasm (light), pop culture nods | Memes, dry wit, self-aware humor |
| **Teaching style** | Show, don't tell. Big visuals. "Look at THIS!" | Guided discovery. "What do you THINK happens?" | Socratic method. "Why do you think that's true?" | Debate partner. "Okay but what about THIS argument?" |
| **Emotional range** | Happy, surprised, gentle sad, excited | + proud, curious, determined | + frustrated (relatably), impressed, competitive | + sarcastic, philosophical, genuinely moved |
| **Visual complexity** | 3-4 colors, round shapes, big features | More detail, sharper edges, animation complexity | Detailed textures, subtle animations, personality in posture | Minimal, sleek, communicates through micro-expressions |
| **Relationship with kid** | Playful puppy / excited friend | Adventure buddy / exploration partner | Study partner / teammate | Trusted advisor / equal |
| **Slang/language** | "Yay!" "Whoa!" "Cool beans!" | "Awesome!" "Check this out!" | "No cap, that's actually wild" "Legit" | Adapts to whatever the kid's generation uses (pulled from cultural context) |

**The transition is gradual, not a switch.** When a parent dials from 6 to 7, the character doesn't suddenly change overnight. Over the next 5-10 sessions, vocabulary shifts, humor evolves, visual elements add detail. The kid barely notices — it just feels like their friend is growing up with them.

## Character Customization — Kid's Choice

At onboarding, the kid could choose:

**Option A — Visual variant (same character, different skin):**
"What kind of [Kin/Syn/Flux] are you?"
- Circuit Fox (orange/amber glow)
- Pulse Cat (blue/purple glow)
- Spark Owl (green/gold glow)
- Byte Wolf (red/silver glow)

**Option B — Color/personality emphasis:**
"What color should [character] be?"
- Choose core glow color → affects the entire app's accent color
- Choose personality trait emphasis: Silly / Curious / Brave / Calm

**Option C — Earned evolution (progression-based):**
The character starts identical for everyone. As the child learns, they unlock visual upgrades — new patterns, new animations, new transformation abilities. This ties character investment directly to learning progress.

**Recommendation:** Combine A and C. Choose a variant at onboarding (emotional investment from minute one), then earn visual evolutions through learning (retention mechanic). The variant choice is permanent-feeling ("this is MY Kin") but the evolution is continuous.
