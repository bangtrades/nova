### Age 4 — picture-first, one-word answers

At four, the child is producing one-word and short-phrase answers
confidently but has almost no stamina for long prompts. Dashy's voice
here is slow, warm, and ends quickly — the child knows it's their
turn within six or seven seconds of the card appearing.

**Dashy's voice for a 4-year-old**
- `promptText` is 1 short sentence, 8–18 words. Dashy speaks as if
  she's trying to remember something: *"I saw a furry little
  animal — what do you call a baby cat?"*. Opening context, then a
  direct question.
- Use everyday words the child has heard dozens of times. No
  newly-learned vocabulary in the *prompt itself* — the prompt's job
  is to *elicit* a word, not *teach* one.
- Include one concrete visual hook in the prompt if the target word
  is a noun: *"The orange thing rabbits love to eat — what's that
  called?"* The picture-in-the-mind is the scaffold.

**Expected responses**
- 1 word or 2-word phrase. *"kitten"*, *"a kitten"*, *"baby cat"*.
- Include the bare noun + the natural article phrasing ("a kitten")
  — four-year-olds often produce both.
- 2–3 entries is typical. More than 3 means the concept isn't
  sharp enough to be a voice-card at this age.

**Celebration for a 4-year-old**
- Short and bright. *"Yes! That's the one!"*, *"We got it!"*.
- No long explanations. Celebration lands and the card moves on.

**Retry for a 4-year-old**
- Gentle + re-model. *"Hmm, let me think… I was picturing a furry
  baby cat. What's that called?"*. Always re-state the hint, because
  four-year-olds lose the original prompt after one attempt.

**Concept types that work at age 4**
- Animal names, food names, color names, body parts, family-words.
- Short common sayings they've heard in picture books.
- Counting to 10 (partial-sequence completion).

**Concept types to AVOID at age 4**
- Anything requiring the child to *produce* a newly-learned word.
- Multi-step sequences (*"what comes after Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday?"*).
- Abstract categories (*"what's a reptile?"*).
