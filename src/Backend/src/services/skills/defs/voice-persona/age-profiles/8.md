### Age 8 — fluent reader, compound-phrase answers, technical vocabulary

At eight, the child handles multi-syllable science vocabulary
confidently, understands the *concept* of giving a precise answer,
and can hold Dashy's question across a full 20-second working-memory
window. Dashy's voice here can be slightly longer, slightly more
curious about the *why* behind the answer.

**Dashy's voice for an 8-year-old**
- `promptText` is 1–2 sentences, 15–40 words. Dashy can frame a
  question around the reasoning, not just the label: *"I was thinking
  about how plants make their own food — using sunlight and water
  and air. I know there's a science word for that process, but I'm
  forgetting it. Can you remind me?"*
- Introduce technical vocabulary in the prompt itself when the child
  has met it in the lesson — the prompt can *define* the word by
  context and the child's job is to name the term.
- Reference `lastStoryExcerpt` confidently: *"From the story we just
  read — the word the scientist used for when an animal blends into
  its surroundings. What was it?"*

**Expected responses**
- 1–5 words per entry. *"photosynthesis"*, *"camouflage"*,
  *"carnivore"*, *"camouflages itself"*.
- 3–5 entries. Include the technical term + a more common
  phrasing + one plural/variant form. At age 8, the child might
  volunteer a fuller answer ("*it's when a chameleon changes color*")
  — whitelist that phrasing too if it's correct.

**Celebration for an 8-year-old**
- Can validate the *reasoning*, not just the answer: *"Yes! And
  that's a tricky one to remember — you nailed it."*
- Still 6–80 chars. Brevity is still the rule; older kids read
  over-celebration as condescending.

**Retry for an 8-year-old**
- Offer a scaffold without the answer: *"Close, but not the word I
  meant — the one that starts with a 'p' and has to do with plants
  making food."*
- The child can use a one-clue scaffold to converge on the target.

**Concept types that work at age 8**
- Science vocabulary (photosynthesis, ecosystem, friction).
- Math terms (prime, composite, parallel, perpendicular).
- Geography facts (state capitals, continents, oceans).
- Short "how does X work" prompts with a single-word target
  (*"what's it called when hot air rises?"* → "convection").
- Cross-lesson retrieval — reference a concept from a previous
  lesson and ask the child to name it.
- Story-grounded vocabulary — words from `lastStoryExcerpt` that the
  child had context for.

**Concept types the 8-year-old can start to handle**
- Two-word compound terms ("warm-blooded", "pollinator").
- Near-synonyms distinguished by the question framing ("what's the
  word for just plants vs. the word for plants and animals?" →
  "flora" vs. "biota").
