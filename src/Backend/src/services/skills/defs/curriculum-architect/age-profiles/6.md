### Age 6 — early-reader lesson with a real arc

At six, the child is reading short sentences, following two-step
reasoning, and holding the thread of a 5-minute lesson. This is the
age where the decomposition starts to look like a real lesson arc:
**hook → teach → apply → check**. The middle expands from one
explanation atom to two or three, and the experiment strategy becomes
a genuine option.

**Sequence shape at age 6**

- Open with a **narrative** atom — a short scene, usually a small
  character the child recognises from prior lessons. The opener
  should set up the *question* the lesson answers.
- Middle atoms (2–3 of them) are some mix of **explanation**,
  **comparison**, and **experiment**. A well-formed age-6 lesson
  teaches the definition in one atom and the contrast in the next
  — that's where the learning lands.
- Optional penultimate **experiment** atom if the concept sorts
  cleanly into 2 buckets — this is where drag-and-drop shines at
  age 6.
- Close with a **quiz** atom (4 options, application-level).

**Atom descriptions**

- 30–200 characters each. 1–2 short sentences. Can include a
  freshly-taught domain word if the prior atom introduced it.
- CVCC vocabulary and two-syllable words freely. One mildly new
  word per atom is fine if its meaning is obvious from context.
- Claim form: the description states what the atom teaches as a
  fact, not as a question. *"Mammals feed their babies with milk."*
  *"Ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water."*

**Prerequisites**

- First atom: zero prerequisites.
- Explanation atoms: one prerequisite (the atom that introduced the
  domain term they use).
- Experiment atoms: list *every* explanation atom that taught the
  rule being applied — a child can't sort by a rule they haven't
  met.
- Quiz atom: lists every middle atom.

**Engagement / learning-value scoring for age 6**

- Narrative opener: engagement 0.8–0.9, learning-value 0.55–0.7.
- Explanation middle: engagement 0.6–0.75, learning-value 0.8–0.9
  (the concept-carriers get the highest learning-value).
- Experiment: engagement 0.8–0.9, learning-value 0.75–0.85 (doing
  IS learning).
- Final quiz: engagement 0.7, learning-value 0.85–0.9.

**Card-type diversity at age 6**

- At least 3 distinct card types across the lesson. A 4-atom lesson
  should hit {story, concept, quiz} at minimum; a 5-atom lesson
  should hit {story, concept, experiment, quiz}. A lesson that goes
  story → concept → concept → concept → quiz is flat even though
  the math checks out — rework one middle atom into an experiment.

**Never at age 6**

- `voice` strategy — plumbing not ready at sprint 12, and 6 is
  borderline anyway.
- More than one comparison atom in a lesson — contrast pairs are
  strong but a lesson of stacked contrasts reads as a taxonomy
  lecture.
- An experiment atom on a concept that doesn't cleanly sort — if
  the child could reasonably put the same item in two bins, the
  experiment is misplaced, switch to `explanation` or `comparison`.
