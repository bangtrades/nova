### Age 4 — short, concrete, one idea per atom

A four-year-old's attention window is 60–90 seconds per card and the
whole lesson should fit in roughly four minutes of engaged time.
That means **fewer atoms, simpler ideas, and strategies that move
fast**. Any atom that forces the child to hold two things in mind at
once has been split badly — pull it apart.

**Sequence shape at age 4**

- Open with a **narrative** atom — a tiny scene or character that
  carries the concept into the child's head. The story IS the hook.
- Middle atoms are **explanation** or very-simple **experiment**
  (never hard sorts). Each explanation atom teaches one concrete
  fact ("leaves are green", "shadows appear when light is blocked").
- Close with a **quiz** atom — three-option, recognition-level only.
  Never close a four-year-old's lesson with a `voice` card at
  sprint 12 — the voice skill isn't plumbed for that age yet.

**Atom descriptions**

- 20–140 characters each. One short sentence. Start with a noun the
  child knows: *"A leaf is the flat green part of a plant."* *"Ice
  is water that got so cold it turned hard."*
- Use present tense, concrete verbs, no conditionals. No "might",
  "could", "would", "depends on". The concept is true or it's not
  yet the concept for this child.
- No domain jargon unless it's the concept word itself. If the atom
  teaches the word "leaf", the description can use "leaf" — but
  don't pile on "chlorophyll", "stem", "petiole" in the same atom.

**Prerequisites**

- First atom: zero prerequisites.
- Middle atoms: exactly one prerequisite (the immediately preceding
  atom) unless the atoms are genuinely parallel (very rare at age 4).
- Final quiz atom: lists every middle atom as a prerequisite. A
  four-year-old can't be quizzed on something they haven't been
  shown.

**Engagement / learning-value scoring for age 4**

- Narrative opener: engagement 0.85–0.95, learning-value 0.5–0.65.
- Explanation middle: engagement 0.6–0.75, learning-value 0.75–0.9.
- Final quiz: engagement 0.7, learning-value 0.85 (it cements).
- Don't cluster — the ranker can't tell which atom to repeat next
  session if they all look the same.

**Topics to keep simple**

- Anything involving multi-step causality (why the seasons change, why
  the moon phases happen) needs to be softened — either drop to 3
  atoms and teach only the first causal step, or pick a narrower
  sub-topic that fits a 4-year-old's world.

**Never at age 4**

- `cause_effect` as the first atom — the child doesn't have scaffolding.
- `voice` strategy anywhere — plumbing not ready.
- More than 3 atoms — attention runs out.
- An atom whose description names a concept not taught by an earlier
  atom in the same lesson.
