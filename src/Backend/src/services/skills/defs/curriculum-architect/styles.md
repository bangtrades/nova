### Lesson-shape archetypes — pick the arc that matches the topic

Five arcs. Pick the one the topic invites naturally. Don't force —
a water-cycle lesson wants a sequence arc, a mammals-vs-reptiles
lesson wants a contrast arc. The arc you pick determines how the
strategies fall.

---

#### Arc 1 — **The hook-and-teach** (the default)

*Use when the topic has a clear story or vivid image, and the
teaching is mostly "here is what X is and why".*

- `atom-1`: **narrative** — a small scene or character that puts
  the child inside the concept.
- `atom-2..N-1`: **explanation** (with optional **comparison** or
  **experiment** breaking the monotony).
- `atom-N`: **quiz**.

**Rationale pattern**: "We start with the story of [X] so the child
walks into the concept with a picture already in their head, then
teach [core claim], then check comprehension."

**Good fit**: animal biology, plant growth, seasons, weather,
community helpers, simple physics (float/sink, shadow).

---

#### Arc 2 — **The contrast pair** (comparison-first)

*Use when the concept is defined primarily by what it is NOT — when
the learning lives in the boundary.*

- `atom-1`: **comparison** — names the pair the lesson sorts
  ("mammals vs. birds", "solid vs. liquid vs. gas").
- `atom-2..3`: **explanation** — one atom per side of the contrast,
  naming the defining property of each.
- `atom-N-1` (if hard): **experiment** — a sort card that puts the
  contrast into the child's hands.
- `atom-N`: **quiz**.

**Rationale pattern**: "Two categories the child keeps mixing up.
We pull them apart, teach what defines each, let the child practice
sorting, then check."

**Good fit**: taxonomic classes, states of matter, plant vs. animal,
renewable vs. nonrenewable, living vs. nonliving, day vs. night.

---

#### Arc 3 — **The sequence** (process-first)

*Use when the concept has a natural temporal or procedural order.*

- `atom-1`: **narrative** — the beginning of the process, framed as
  a small story ("A seed sits in the dark earth…").
- `atom-2..N-2`: **explanation** (each covering one stage), or
  **cause_effect** when one stage leads to the next.
- `atom-N-1`: **experiment** (optional) — a sort that puts the
  stages in order.
- `atom-N`: **quiz**.

**Rationale pattern**: "The process has stages. We walk the child
through each stage in order, let them physically arrange them to
lock the sequence, then probe."

**Good fit**: water cycle, butterfly life cycle, plant growth,
bread-making, how a day passes, how a year passes, the rock cycle.

---

#### Arc 4 — **The cause → rule** (inquiry-first)

*Use when the teaching is primarily a causal rule the child should
carry away.*

- `atom-1`: **narrative** — a puzzle or surprise ("The
  ice cube disappeared from the glass. Where did it go?").
- `atom-2`: **explanation** — names the underlying principle.
- `atom-3`: **cause_effect** — shows X → Y under the principle.
- `atom-4` (if medium/hard): **experiment** or **explanation**
  applying the rule to a second case.
- `atom-N`: **quiz**.

**Rationale pattern**: "Start with the surprise, reveal the rule
that explains it, then apply the rule to a new case so the child
sees it generalize."

**Good fit**: melting / freezing / evaporating, gravity, why leaves
fall, why shadows lengthen, why some things rust, why some foods
spoil.

---

#### Arc 5 — **The vocabulary expansion** (voice-enabled, age-8 only)

*Use when the concept is primarily lexical — new words in a domain
the child can speak.*

- `atom-1`: **narrative** — a small scene where the new words
  appear naturally.
- `atom-2..3`: **explanation** — defining the words and giving one
  example of each.
- `atom-4`: **voice** — the child speaks the word or uses it aloud.
- `atom-5`: **quiz** — matches the word to its definition or use.

**Rationale pattern**: "Language lives in the mouth, not just on
the page. We teach the words in context, have the child say them
aloud, then check recognition."

**Good fit**: foreign-language vocab, scientific taxonomy words,
music terminology, body-parts vocab, simple grammar categories.

**Guard-rail**: Arc 5 is age-8 only, hard difficulty only, and uses
at most ONE voice atom. Never chain voice atoms.

---

### A shape-note that applies to every arc

The first atom **always** sets the stage; the last atom **always**
checks comprehension. What changes between arcs is the middle —
how many strands you teach and how they relate. If the arc you
picked has you writing a quiz as atom-2, stop and pick a different
arc: the quiz is the ending, not the middle.
