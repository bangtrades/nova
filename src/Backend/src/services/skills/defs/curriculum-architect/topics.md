### Topic-type guidance — strategy mixes that typically work

The topic's domain shape pre-selects which strategies carry weight.
Use this as a starting point, not a straitjacket — if your topic
sits between two types, pick the mix that best serves what you
want the child to walk away with.

---

#### Life-sciences topics (animals, plants, ecosystems, biology)

**Natural arc**: Arc 1 (hook-and-teach) or Arc 2 (contrast pair).

**Strategy mix that usually works**:
- Opener: **narrative** — animals and plants have obvious stories
  and the child is primed to care.
- Middle: 1–2 **explanation** atoms for the core claim (what
  the category is, what it needs, how it behaves).
- Optional: **comparison** when the topic is inherently
  comparative (mammal vs. bird, predator vs. prey).
- Late: **experiment** when the category sorts cleanly (mammal /
  not-mammal, herbivore / carnivore / omnivore).
- Close: **quiz**.

**Watch-out**: Life sciences over-index on narrative — make sure
the explanation atoms actually carry the defining claim, not just
another story about the animal.

---

#### Physical-sciences topics (matter, energy, forces, weather)

**Natural arc**: Arc 3 (sequence) for cyclic topics, Arc 4 (cause→
rule) for causal topics.

**Strategy mix**:
- Opener: **narrative** with a small puzzle or surprise (the
  ice-cube-vanishing, the ball-that-wouldn't-sink).
- Middle: **explanation** of the principle, followed by
  **cause_effect** showing the principle in action.
- Optional: **experiment** sorting things by the physical
  property (floats / sinks, magnetic / not).
- Close: **quiz** with at least one distractor that probes a
  common misconception.

**Watch-out**: Physical sciences tempt over-use of cause_effect at
age 4–6 — the child may not yet have the scaffolding to reason
causally. Drop to a single cause_effect atom and lean on
explanation at younger ages.

---

#### Earth & space topics (seasons, weather, solar system, geology)

**Natural arc**: Arc 3 (sequence) for cycles, Arc 1 (hook-and-teach)
for descriptive.

**Strategy mix**:
- Opener: **narrative** — a scene the child recognises ("It's
  getting dark earlier each day…") or a place they can picture.
- Middle: **explanation** atoms naming the cyclic structure OR
  the descriptive fact.
- Optional: **experiment** sorting by season or by type of weather.
- Close: **quiz**.

**Watch-out**: Scale is the killer — a four-year-old can't hold
"the earth orbits the sun once per year" as a meaningful claim
without an orbit visualized. When the scale is too big, pick a
narrower sub-topic (what happens in *winter*, rather than why
seasons exist).

---

#### Math topics (numbers, shapes, patterns, measurement)

**Natural arc**: Arc 2 (contrast pair) or Arc 4 (cause→rule).

**Strategy mix**:
- Opener: **narrative** (sparingly — math doesn't always narrate
  well at ages 4–6) or a **comparison** atom framing the contrast
  (even vs. odd, more vs. less, straight vs. curved).
- Middle: **explanation** of the rule or definition.
- Optional: **experiment** sorting (odd/even, bigger/smaller,
  prime/composite at age 8).
- Close: **quiz** with the numeric form the rule applies to.

**Watch-out**: Math topics are the biggest temptation for pure-quiz
lessons. Resist — a lesson of four quiz atoms teaches worksheet
stamina, not mathematical understanding. Keep at least one
explanation atom carrying the *why*.

---

#### Language & literacy topics (phonics, vocabulary, grammar, poetry)

**Natural arc**: Arc 1 (hook-and-teach) or Arc 5 (vocabulary
expansion, age-8 only).

**Strategy mix**:
- Opener: **narrative** — a rhyme, a short poem, a scene.
- Middle: **explanation** atoms naming the rule or word.
- Optional: **voice** (age 8 only) — the child says the word or
  reads the line aloud.
- Close: **quiz** recognizing the target.

**Watch-out**: Voice is tempting for every language lesson — resist
at age 4–6 where the plumbing isn't ready. At age 8, keep voice to
one atom max per lesson.

---

#### Social-studies topics (community, history, geography, civics)

**Natural arc**: Arc 1 (hook-and-teach) or Arc 3 (sequence, for
historical timelines).

**Strategy mix**:
- Opener: **narrative** — a small story about a person or place.
- Middle: **explanation** atoms naming the concept (what a
  community helper does, what a country is, what a map shows).
- Optional: **comparison** (urban vs. rural, past vs. present).
- Close: **quiz** recognizing the target concept.

**Watch-out**: History at ages 4–6 runs into the same scale problem
as astronomy — "a long time ago" is a blur. Anchor history topics
to the child's own life where possible ("your grandparents'
grandparents" rather than "the 1800s").

---

### A note on topic-type ambiguity

Many topics sit at the intersection of two types. A lesson on *why
leaves are green* is both life sciences (what leaves do) and
physical sciences (the light / pigment interaction). When in doubt:
pick the type that matches the **primary learning-value atom** —
the one claim the lesson most needs the child to walk away with.
If the takeaway is "leaves are plants' food factories", life
sciences leads; if the takeaway is "pigments absorb certain
colors", physical sciences leads.

When unsure, default to **Arc 1** with strategy mix
narrative → explanation → explanation → quiz, then adjust one
middle atom to experiment or comparison if the topic calls for it.
