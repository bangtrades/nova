### Age 6 — early reader, two-attribute sorts

At six, the child is reading short labels independently and can hold
**two** attributes in mind at once ("living things that fly" vs.
"living things that don't"). They're starting to notice counterexamples
— the bat that flies but isn't a bird, the penguin that's a bird but
swims. That's a feature, not a bug: well-chosen items at this age
teach the child that the category is what defines membership, not a
superficial trait.

**Voice for the child-facing copy**
- Instructions are 1–2 short sentences, 12–24 words. *"Some of these
  things float and some sink. Drag each one to the right bin."*
- Questions like *"Where does each one belong?"* are fine.
- Use CVCC and two-syllable words freely. Introduce one mildly new
  word per card if the picture makes its meaning obvious.
- Title is 2–6 words, title case: *"Which Ones Float?"*, *"Sort by
  Season"*, *"What Makes a Mammal?"*.

**Target (bin) labels**
- 1–3 words. *"Floats"*, *"Sinks"*. *"Mammal"*, *"Not a mammal"*.
  *"Solid"*, *"Liquid"*. *"Warm-blooded"*, *"Cold-blooded"* if the
  concept was just taught.
- Short phrases are OK if they name the category cleanly: *"Lives in
  water"*, *"Has six legs"*.

**Item (chip) labels**
- 1–3 words. Concrete everyday objects/animals/foods/actions.
  *"Apple"*, *"Bouncy ball"*, *"Toy car"*, *"Raincoat"*.
- It's OK to include one mildly tricky item that teaches the category
  (a bat in mammals, a dolphin in mammals). Tricky items should still
  have **exactly one** right home — no ambiguity.

**Safe materials vocabulary**
- ✅ OK to name: everything from age 4, plus: magnifying glass (adult
  supervised), measuring cup, kitchen scale, ruler, small plants,
  seeds, soil, thermometer, flashlight, batteries **in a case**
  (already-assembled toys), paper clips, string, rubber bands (for
  tying — not stretching at eyes).
- 🚫 Do NOT name at age 6: scissors with pointed tips (round-tipped
  kid scissors are OK), sewing needles, matches, lighters, loose
  button batteries, bleach, cleaning chemicals, stove burners, hot
  glue guns, soldering, raw meat handling.

**Concepts this age usually CAN classify**
- Living/nonliving, plant/animal, mammal/bird/fish, floats/sinks,
  solid/liquid/gas (intro level), push/pull, magnetic/not,
  odd/even for single-digit numbers, day/night activities,
  seasons, needs vs. wants (simple).

**Concepts this age usually CANNOT classify**
- Multi-step cause-and-effect chains, abstract economic categories,
  chemical reactions beyond mixing/dissolving, geological time,
  probability, most higher-order math.
