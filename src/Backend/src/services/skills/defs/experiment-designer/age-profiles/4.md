### Age 4 — picture-first, single-attribute sorts

At four, the child is classifying by the most visible property of a thing:
color, shape, where it lives, what it does. Abstract categories
("renewable resources", "even numbers") are off the table. The sort has
to hinge on something a four-year-old can see in their head.

**Voice for the child-facing copy**
- Instructions are **one short sentence**, 10–16 words. Start with a
  verb the child has physically done: *"Drag each one into the right
  box."* / *"Put each animal where it lives."*
- Use present tense only. No conditionals, no "would".
- Title is 2–4 words. Use concrete nouns the child knows: *"Sort the
  Animals"*, *"Find Each Home"*, *"Sky, Land, Water"*.
- No sarcasm, no rhetorical questions, no exclamation points unless
  the whole app is celebrating.

**Target (bin) labels**
- 1–2 words each. *"Sky"*, *"Water"*, *"Land"*. *"Hot"*, *"Cold"*.
  *"Floats"*, *"Sinks"*.
- Labels name the category *directly* — never indirect ("Things that
  swim") at this age. The word on the bin is the property.

**Item (chip) labels**
- 1 word preferred, 2 words maximum. *"Fish"*, *"Bird"*, *"Ice cube"*.
- Items the child has seen in person or in picture books. No fictional
  creatures, no brand names, no abstractions. *"Bird"* ✅
  / *"Raptor"* 🚫 / *"Tweety"* 🚫.

**Safe materials vocabulary (if the concept touches physical
experiments)**
- ✅ OK to name: water, ice, warm water, cold water, food, fruit,
  leaves, rocks, sand, soft toys, crayons, a blanket, pillow,
  bathtub, cup, bowl, spoon.
- 🚫 Do NOT name at age 4: scissors, knives, needles, matches,
  lighters, batteries, magnets smaller than a fist, small beads,
  balloons, plastic bags, bleach, any cleaning product, stove, oven,
  boiling anything, raw eggs, pills, medicine, electrical outlets,
  cords.

**Concepts this age usually CAN classify**
- Home/habitat ("where does each animal live"), basic properties
  ("hot/cold", "big/small", "fast/slow"), simple sequences ("what
  happens first/next"), and "alive vs not alive" in its simplest
  form.

**Concepts this age usually CANNOT classify (pick a different probe)**
- Abstract math (even/odd, prime), time spans longer than day/night,
  economic categories, moral judgments, comparative energy
  (renewable vs. nonrenewable), chemistry beyond "ice melts".
