### Difficulty: hard — 5 items × 3 bins

A three-way sort. The concept has to cleanly partition into three
meaningful categories — if you can't name three distinct buckets
that each hold at least one prototypical item and one counterexample,
**pick an easier difficulty** rather than force a third bin.

**Counts**
- `dragItems.length` = **5**
- `dropTargets.length` = **3**
- Distribution: each bin gets at least **1** item. Preferred is 2/2/1
  or 2/1/2. Never let any bin be empty.

**Cognitive load**
- Transfer-level. The child must apply the rule to novel items they
  haven't seen in class, including at least one item where the
  surface feature points one way and the definition points another.
- Expect 5–10 seconds per item, longer for the counterexamples.

**Bin design for hard**
- Three mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive categories for this
  concept: *solid / liquid / gas*, *producer / consumer / decomposer*,
  *renewable / nonrenewable / inexhaustible*, *herbivore / carnivore /
  omnivore*, *prime / composite / neither (just "1")*.
- Each target label is a domain term the child has met in the
  curriculum. One-to-four words.
- The `rationalePerTarget` entry for each bin should name the
  defining property, not just restate the label.

**Item design for hard**
- Mix of prototypical and tricky:
    - 2 prototypical items (clearly belong to one bin on first glance)
    - 2 items that require the child to apply the rule carefully
    - 1 item that's a classic counterexample — the *"tomato is a
      fruit"* type, the *"whale is a mammal"* type, the *"mushroom
      isn't a plant"* type.
- Still: every item belongs in **exactly one** bin. No "it could go
  in either" items, ever. Tricky is fine; ambiguous breaks the card.
