### Difficulty: easy — 3 items × 2 bins

The simplest sort that still asks the child to discriminate. Every
bin is obvious from its label; every item has a screaming-obvious
right home. The point is to establish that the child understands
the *format* of the sort AND has the baseline grasp of the concept.

**Counts**
- `dragItems.length` = **3**
- `dropTargets.length` = **2**
- Distribution: one bin gets **2** items, the other gets **1**. Never
  3/0 (that's not a sort). Never 1/2 with the 1 being a gotcha.

**Cognitive load**
- Recognition-level. The child should be able to classify each item in
  under 2 seconds. If they hesitate at easy, the sort is too hard or
  the item is poorly chosen.
- No counterexamples, no trick items. Everything is prototypical:
  if sorting animals by habitat, use *fish* for water, *eagle* for
  sky, *cow* for land — not *penguin*, *flying fish*, *platypus*.

**Bin design for easy**
- Two mutually exclusive categories that together cover the child's
  mental world of the concept: *floats / sinks*, *alive / not alive*,
  *hot / cold*, *day / night*. Don't use *"land / sky"* without
  thinking about where fish go — pick bins that together cover all
  three items cleanly.

**Item design for easy**
- Each item is a **textbook example** of exactly one bin. No edge
  cases, no dual-property items.
- All three items come from the child's everyday picture-book world.
