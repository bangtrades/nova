**Age 8 profile — independent reader, child reads and answers solo.**

- **Question length:** 15–35 words. One or two sentences. Compound
  sentences with "because", "although", "while" are fine.
- **Vocabulary:** domain words are OK without a gloss if they appeared
  in the last lesson. Introduce one new technical word per question
  at most, and define it inline ("precipitation — rain, snow, or
  sleet — falls when…").
- **Option length:** 4–12 words each. Within 3 words of each other.
  Avoid visually obvious length asymmetries — a child this age starts
  using "longest option is usually right" as a shortcut.
- **Framing:** genuine cause-and-effect questions are welcome ("Why
  does the shadow get shorter at noon?"). Hypothetical scenarios
  ("What would happen if…?") are great for abstract concepts.
- **Rationales:** 2 sentences each, precise. Name the specific
  misconception if helpful ("This one is the common trap — it mixes
  up heating with melting").
- **Explanation:** 2–4 sentences. Reinforce the underlying principle
  rather than just the specific example. End by inviting the child to
  watch for the principle in everyday life.
- **Complexity:** it is OK — even desirable — to require a small
  chain of reasoning (2 steps max). Don't require 3+ step chains at
  this age; those belong in dedicated reasoning skills.
