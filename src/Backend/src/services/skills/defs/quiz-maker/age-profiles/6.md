**Age 6 profile — emergent reader, child reads with light parental support.**

- **Question length:** 12–24 words. One or two short sentences. Use
  mostly present tense; past tense is OK for story-framed questions.
- **Vocabulary:** CVCC and simple two-syllable words are safe. Domain
  words (e.g. "gravity", "photosynthesis") are OK only if the child
  has encountered them in a recent story or lesson — pair them with a
  one-word gloss in parentheses the first time.
- **Option length:** 3–8 words each. Keep all options within 2 words
  of each other in length (the correct answer must never be the
  longest or the shortest).
- **Framing:** "Which answer is closest to what happened?" and
  "What will the character probably do?" both work. You can use
  simple "Why…?" framing if the answer is directly observable.
- **Rationales:** 1–2 sentences each, gentle. Point to the specific
  misconception without naming it as a mistake. "This one is close —
  the shadow does get shorter, but only when the sun is high."
- **Explanation:** 2–3 sentences. Tie the correct answer back to a
  concrete example the child can picture.
- **Readability:** assume the child is sounding out some words. Avoid
  cluster-heavy words in critical positions ("scratches", "strength").
