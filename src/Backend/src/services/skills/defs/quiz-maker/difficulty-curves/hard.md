**Hard — 5 options. Transfer-level probe.**

- **Option count:** exactly 5.
- **Distractor gap:** every option represents a coherent, internally
  consistent interpretation. The correct answer is the *only* one
  that survives all the constraints in the question.
- **Cognitive load:** transfer. The question presents a scenario in
  a domain the child has not studied directly, and asks them to apply
  the concept from the domain they *have* studied.
- **Distractor design:**
    1. **Surface-correct, deep-wrong** — passes casual inspection.
    2. **Right mechanism, wrong direction** — the causal arrow is
       reversed.
    3. **Right mechanism, wrong scope** — correct for the general
       case but ignores a specific constraint named in the question.
    4. **Over-generalization** — applies the concept where it
       shouldn't apply (e.g., treating gravity the same in water as
       in air).
- **Calibration target:** a child who has genuinely mastered the
  concept and can transfer it to a new context should still only
  score 40–55% on this. Hard questions are designed to distinguish
  mastery from surface understanding; frequent hard-level success
  over many sessions is the strongest mastery signal we collect.
- **Formatting discipline:** options at this level tend to drift in
  length. Keep them all within 3 words of the shortest option. Never
  let the correct answer become the longest one.
