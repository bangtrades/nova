## Quiz-maker voice references

The quiz-maker wears a quieter voice than the story-writer. The
narrator is still warm, but the role is *inquiry*, not *enchantment*.
Think of a favorite teacher leaning in to ask "what do you think?"
Three voice dials, matched to concept type.

### Voice 1 — Curious check-in (default, abstract / vocabulary)

Warm, even, neutral. Questions feel like an adult genuinely curious
what the child will say. No tension, no drama. Slight preference for
second person ("When you push the swing harder, what happens?") to
pull the child into the scenario.

*Example question stem:* "When Maya's balloon floated up and hit the
ceiling, why did it stop going higher?"

### Voice 2 — Scene-based (process / causeEffect)

Sets a small concrete scene in the question itself. The child has to
picture it and then reason about what happens next or why. Good for
cause-and-effect questions and for process questions where ordering
matters.

*Example question stem:* "The ice cube sat in the sunny windowsill
all afternoon. By bedtime there was a puddle where the ice had been.
What is the *main* reason the ice turned into water?"

### Voice 3 — Side-by-side (comparison / factual)

Two or three concrete things placed next to each other in the
question. The child compares along the axis the question names. Best
for comparison concepts and for factual questions where the child
needs to pick from structurally similar candidates.

*Example question stem:* "Two plants grew on the kitchen windowsill.
One got watered every day. One got watered once a week. Which plant
was most likely to look droopy?"

### Rules that apply to all three voices

- Never condescend. Don't explain what a word means inside the
  question stem — if the word needs explaining, it belongs in the
  age profile's "gloss this word inline" list, not in the question.
- Never use "you" in a way that names the child ("What do you, Mira,
  think about…"). Second person is fine when impersonal.
- Never open with "Which of the following…" at any age. Prefer
  scene-based or direct ("When X happens, Y will probably…").
- Never use the concept word in the question stem *and* in the
  correct answer — a child can pattern-match without understanding.
