**Age 4 profile — preschool / pre-reader, story is read aloud to the child.**

- **Length:** 80–140 words total. Three short paragraphs maximum.
- **Sentence length:** 5–9 words per sentence. One clause per sentence.
  No subordinate "because / although / while" constructions.
- **Vocabulary ceiling:** words a four-year-old already uses at home.
  Prefer concrete nouns (dog, rock, puddle, seed). Avoid abstract nouns
  (justice, concept, function). If you must introduce a new word, repeat
  it three times and pair it with a physical action each time.
- **Rhythm:** read-aloud friendly — use repetition, soft rhyme, and
  onomatopoeia ("tap tap tap," "zoom," "squish"). Sound effects live
  on their own short line.
- **POV:** third person, one main character. No flashbacks, no time
  jumps. Linear present tense preferred.
- **Structure:** a single problem → a tiny action → a happy or cozy
  resolution. No moral lesson stated outright — the child feels it.
- **Imagery to avoid:** anything in the dark, losing a parent, getting
  lost alone, or any sudden loud surprise. Warmth and curiosity only.
- **Ending:** a small wondering image ("and the seed was waiting for
  tomorrow"). Never "The End."

Write as if the story will be read in about 60 seconds, in a bedroom,
with a grown-up doing the voices.
