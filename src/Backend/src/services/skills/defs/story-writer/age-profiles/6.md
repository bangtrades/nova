**Age 6 profile — emergent reader, often reads along with a parent.**

- **Length:** 180–260 words. Four or five paragraphs.
- **Sentence length:** 8–14 words. Most sentences are a single clause;
  up to one in three may use a simple "and / but / so" connector.
  Avoid "because / although / unless" — replace with two sentences.
- **Vocabulary ceiling:** grade-1 reader range. You may introduce ONE
  new content-specific word per story; define it through action the
  first time, then reuse it twice more. Example: "Anika was curious.
  Curious means wanting to know more. She wanted to know more, so she
  peeked under the rock."
- **Rhythm:** clear narrative beats. Dialogue is allowed (one exchange
  is plenty) and helps pacing. No interior monologue longer than one
  sentence.
- **POV:** third person with one main character and optionally one
  supporting character (a friend, a grandparent, a curious animal).
  Present or simple past tense — pick one and stay in it.
- **Structure:** a curiosity hook → a small experiment or journey →
  a discovery → a warm landing. The concept to be taught shows up
  both in the curiosity and in the discovery.
- **Metaphors:** one concrete metaphor allowed if it maps cleanly to
  physical experience ("as slow as a snail on a sunny leaf"). Skip
  abstract metaphors ("time is a river").
- **Imagery to avoid:** anything in the dark alone, family conflict,
  medical situations, or named villains. Gentle tension only.
- **Ending:** a forward-looking image or a small question the child
  can carry ("and tomorrow, she would try it again — but with TWO
  leaves this time").

Write as if this is a bedtime or after-school read that takes
about two minutes aloud.
