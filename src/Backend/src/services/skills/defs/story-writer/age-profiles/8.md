**Age 8 profile — independent reader, comfortable with short chapter
books and non-fiction picture books.**

- **Length:** 320–460 words. Five to eight paragraphs.
- **Sentence length:** average 12–18 words. Mix simple and complex
  sentences freely — up to one dependent clause per sentence. Rhythm
  should vary; avoid stacking three long sentences in a row.
- **Vocabulary ceiling:** late-grade-2 / early-grade-3. You may
  introduce TWO new content-specific words; define each through
  context the first time, reuse each at least twice. Precise verbs
  are encouraged ("peered, examined, sketched, traced, whispered").
- **Rhythm:** more developed scene work. Dialogue is allowed, with
  up to three exchanges; interior thought is allowed in brief. Show
  the character thinking through the problem on the page — the
  *how* of their thinking is part of the lesson.
- **POV:** third-person limited is the default. One main character
  plus up to two supporting characters. Past tense works well.
- **Structure:** a hook that sets up a puzzle → an attempted answer
  that falls short → a reframe or small insight → a second attempt
  that works → a reflective beat. The concept should carry the
  reframe — that is the moment the child feels the idea clicking.
- **Metaphors:** up to two metaphors allowed. At least one should be
  concrete and grounded in the character's world; the other may be
  slightly more abstract ("his attention felt like a dimmer switch
  someone had turned down").
- **Themes permitted:** friendship, curiosity, persistence, kindness
  across differences, the joy of being wrong and learning from it,
  observation of nature. Still avoid mortal danger, grief,
  medical emergencies, adult romance, and named antagonists.
- **Ending:** a reflective image or a line that invites a follow-up
  question. The ending should land the insight without announcing
  it ("she looked at the puddle differently now").

Write as if this is a piece a third-grader might read alone on a
rainy Saturday — sharp enough to hold attention, warm enough to
feel like a friend.
