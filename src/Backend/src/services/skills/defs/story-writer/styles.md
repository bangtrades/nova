## Voice references

Three different voices Novai's story-writer can wear. Choose the one
that best fits the concept and the child's interests. They are all
warm — what changes is the pacing and the relationship with wonder.

### Voice 1 — Warm and cozy (default)

Soft, close, familiar. The narrator feels like a favorite aunt or
grandparent telling a story with a blanket over their knees.
Grounded in small sensory detail (the smell of toast, the way a
cat curls into a lap, the crunch of leaves). Pace is unhurried.
Sentences often begin with concrete nouns.

*Example opening:* "The little brown owl had lived in the crooked
oak for as long as any of the squirrels could remember. Every
evening, she puffed her feathers and listened to the wind."

### Voice 2 — Curious and observant

The narrator notices things other narrators would skip — the way
light falls through a leaf, the second-to-last step on a staircase
that always creaks. Tone is gently analytical but never dry. Good
for process concepts (how things work, why things happen).

*Example opening:* "If you had been standing in Mira's kitchen
that Tuesday morning, you would have noticed something odd — the
tea in her mug had begun to swirl, all on its own, with no spoon
in sight."

### Voice 3 — Adventurous and bright

Quicker pulse, bigger gestures, a little humor. The narrator
delights in the unexpected turn. Best for causeEffect and
abstract concepts where the "aha" wants a bit of comedy.

*Example opening:* "Theo had exactly twenty-three seconds to
solve the problem, and he was using every single one of them to
stare at the ceiling. Which, it turned out, was exactly the
right place to look."

### Voice rules that apply to all three

- Never condescend. A four-year-old and an eight-year-old can both
  tell when a story is looking down at them.
- Don't over-explain. The concept should be felt before it's named.
- Use proper names sparingly — one or two characters named by the
  end of the story is plenty.
- Avoid contemporary brand references and pop-culture callouts.
